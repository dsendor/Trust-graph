# Round 2 attribution: eight more Astro2010 bets

Companion to `actors-round2-astro.csv` (90 rows: 8 committee, 58 person, 24 agency; 82
confident, 8 guess). Written 2026-10-02. Same columns and conventions as `actors-draft.csv`.
Named people stay in these draft files only (CLAUDE.md rule 7). The Astro2010 committee
member rows (bet_id blank) already exist in `actors-draft.csv` and are not repeated.

Bets covered: astro2010-explorer, astro2010-lisa, astro2010-new-worlds-tech,
astro2010-inflation-probe-tech, astro2010-msip, astro2010-gsmt, astro2010-acta, astro2010-ccat.

Per bet: committee row, Blandford chair row, the chair of the panel that ranked it (from the
NAP 12951 front matter, read via the www.nationalacademies.org redirect), agency rows (funder
plus later actions with dates), and champions at ranking from arXiv white papers or RFI
responses, with later leaders only where a page read names them.

Panel chairs used: Alan Dressler (Electromagnetic Observations from Space) for Explorer, New
Worlds tech and Inflation Probe tech; Jacqueline Hewitt (Particle Astrophysics and
Gravitation) for LISA and ACTA; Patrick Osmer (Optical and Infrared from the Ground) for GSMT
and MSIP; Neal Evans (Radio, Millimeter and Submillimeter) for CCAT. The front matter also
gives vice chairs: Skrutskie (OIR), Moran (RMS), none printed for EOS and PAG.

## Corrections to the brief

- arXiv:0902.4010 is not the AGIS white paper; it is a hep-th paper (van Rees, "Real-time
  gauge/gravity duality"). The AGIS community case on arXiv is arXiv:0810.0444 (APS DAP white
  paper, October 2008, organizers Buckley, Byrum, Dingus, Falcone, Kaaret, Krawczynski, Pohl,
  Vassiliev, Williams). The AGIS Astro2010 RFI response itself was not found on arXiv.
- The LISA Astro2010 RFI response lives on lisa.nasa.gov (blocked). The open champion
  statement is arXiv:0903.0103 (Prince for the LISA International Science Team) plus
  arXiv:0903.0100 (Schutz et al.) and arXiv:0902.1527 (Bloom, Holz, Hughes, Menou).
- The GMT and TMT RFI responses are not on arXiv. GMT and TMT people are sourced from site
  testing papers (arXiv:1101.2340, arXiv:0904.1183) and citations, so their titles are
  `guess`.
- The CCAT Astro2010 white paper (Giovanelli et al. 2009) is listed on submm.caltech.edu
  (not an allowed host) and is not on arXiv; the 2006 and 2007 concept papers are used.
- The Levine et al. Astro2010 technology white paper ("Overview of Technologies for Direct
  Optical Imaging of Exoplanets") is not on arXiv; arXiv:0911.3200 (TPF-C flight baseline,
  Levine lead) and arXiv:0904.0941 (ATLAST, Postman lead) are used.
- MSIP has no project champion: it was a committee-generated program. No person rows beyond
  the committee, chair and panel chair.

## Per bet

### astro2010-explorer

Agency: NASA sole funder (NWNH Table ES.5, $463M). Midterm chapter 4: first astrophysics
Explorer AO of the decade released September 2014 (an earlier AO did not proceed to
selection); NASA plan of at least four AOs 2012-2021 (Recommendation 4-3). Champions:
arXiv:0911.3383 "A Vigorous Explorer Program" (2009-11-17), lead author Martin Elvis (SAO),
49 co-authors including Gehrels, Mather, Weisskopf, Ricker, Traub. Later Explorer PI roles
(Weisskopf IXPE, Ricker TESS) are not verified on an allowed host and are not recorded.

### astro2010-lisa

Agency: NASA (assumed 50 percent partner, NWNH ES.5). Midterm chapter 4: in 2011 the
ESA/NASA co-equal partnership was dissolved (JWST overrun plus LISA ranked behind WFIRST);
U.S. LISA funding fell from about $3M/yr to about $0.3M/yr; by 2016 NASA was authorized to
plan a 10 percent ($150M) stake in eLISA; ESA L3 Gravitational Universe theme, launch in the
2030s. Later: arXiv:2601.15365 (LISA launch mid-2035, ESA with NASA and member states);
arXiv:2012.02650 (NASA LISA Study Team, NASA as junior partner). Champions at ranking:
arXiv:0903.0103 (2009-02-28) "Lead Author: Tom Prince (Caltech/JPL) for Members of the LISA
International Science Team", listing Bender, Binetruy, Buchman, Cerdonio, Cutler, Cruise,
Centrella, Danzmann, Finn, Hough, Gundlach, Jennrich, Hogan, Jetzer, Hughes, Lobo, Madau,
Mellier, Phinney, Schutz, Richstone, Sumner, Stebbins, Vinet, Thorne, Vitale. Later leaders:
Danzmann lead proposer of the L3 proposal (arXiv:1702.00786, January 2017); ESA Definition
Study Report (arXiv:2402.07571, September 2023) LISA Science Study Team: Colpi, Danzmann,
Hewitson, Holley-Bockelmann, Jetzer, Nelemans, Petiteau, Shoemaker, Sopuerta, Stebbins,
Tanvir, Ward, Weber, plus "Ira Thorpe (NASA Study Scientist)". NASA project scientist titles
for Stebbins and the 2024 adoption date are snippets only.

### astro2010-new-worlds-tech

Agency: NASA sole funder (NWNH ES.4, $100M-$200M). Midterm chapter 4: SAT program over $64M
in the first half of the decade across exoplanet, CMB, GW and X-ray; at NASA's direction the
2015 SDT made the coronagraph a baseline (descopable) part of WFIRST-AFTA, $0.35B of
$2.0B-$2.3B (FY2015$). Champions: Marie Levine lead author of arXiv:0911.3200 (TPF-C flight
baseline, 2009-11-17; co-authors Lisman, Shaklan, Kasting, Traub, Guyon, Clampin and 49
more); Marc Postman lead author of arXiv:0904.0941 (ATLAST technology roadmap, 2009-04-06);
Webster Cash (New Worlds Observer starshade, `guess`: the Astro2010 submission is from a
snippet citing arXiv:2602.14823; arXiv:1204.6063 read). Later: Vanessa Bailey lead author of
the Roman Coronagraph Instrument overview (arXiv:2309.08672, 2023; local cgi.txt).

### astro2010-inflation-probe-tech

Agency: NASA sole funder (NWNH ES.4, $60M, range $60M-$200M). Midterm chapter 4: program
contingent on a B-mode detection that never came; current efforts balloon flights and the
proposed ground-based CMB-S4. Champions: arXiv:0811.3919 CMBPol inflation study (2008-11-24,
v2 2009-03-14), co-lead authors Daniel Baumann and Mark G. Jackson, "included into a larger
document to be submitted to the Decadal survey at the end of 2008"; arXiv:0811.3915
foregrounds study, lead author Joanna Dunkley; arXiv:0906.1188 EPIC-IM study (2009-06-04)
"James Bock (PI) JPL/Caltech" with a 44-name study team including Hanany, Lee, Meyer,
Lawrence, Zmuidzinas, and the statement "As instructed by the decadal review, we assume a
start in October 2010"; arXiv:0805.4207 EPIC Phase 1 (Bock, Cooray, Hanany, Keating, Lee et
al.). Later: Hanany first author of PICO (arXiv:1908.07495, 2019). Cross-link: Adrian Lee is
also a CMB-S4 champion in `actors-draft.csv`.

### astro2010-msip

Agency: NSF-AST funder (NWNH ES.3, $93M-$200M). Midterm chapter 3: 2012 Portfolio Review;
first MSIP call June 2013 (38 pre-proposals requesting $400M, six awards $27.1M); Finding
3-3: implemented only by subsuming URO, TSIP, ReSTAR and divesting facilities; mid-scale
funding $31M (FY2010) to $15.5M (FY2015), $21M (FY2016). No champion rows (committee
program); the arXiv API search for Astro2010 mid-scale white papers returned nothing.

### astro2010-gsmt

Agency: NSF funder (NWNH ES.3, about 25 percent federal share of GMT $1.1B or TMT $1.4B).
Midterm Finding 3-5 (2016): NSF budget constraints prevented partner selection; only a
partnership-planning solicitation, answered by TMT (about 20 percent MREFC scenario). AIP FYI
2024-02-27: National Science Board $1.6B ceiling, down-select anticipated. AIP FYI
2024-12-16: external review commissioned by NSF Director Panchanathan. AIP FYI week of
2025-06-02: GMT advanced to final design without a construction commitment, TMT dropped.
Champions (all `guess` on titles): Patrick McCarthy and Matt Johns (GMTO, arXiv:1101.2340,
GMT site testing final report 2010, GMT CoDR 2006, "The Giant Magellan Telescope" SPIE 8444
2012 cited in arXiv:2606.11253); Jerry Nelson and Gary Sanders (TMT, arXiv:0904.1183 for
Nelson; "The Thirty Meter Telescope (TMT) project" SPIE 7012 2008 cited in arXiv:2606.11253);
Warren Skidmore (TMT Observatory Corporation in 2009, lead author of arXiv:1806.02481 for
TMT International Observatory in 2018, confident on authorship).

### astro2010-acta

Agency: DOE and NSF funders (NWNH ES.3, $100M U.S. share split DOE, NSF-PHY, NSF-AST). P5
2014 p. 15 (local sources/p5-2014.txt): invest in CTA as a small project only if NSF
Astronomy funding is obtained; scope could halve. Midterm chapter 3 (2016): U.S. plan reduced
to the NSF Astronomy and Physics mid-scale level; CTA sites chosen 2015 (Paranal, La Palma).
NSF funded the prototype Schwarzschild-Couder Telescope through MRI award 1229792
(arXiv:1709.06324 acknowledgements); outcomes row says the SCT is still only a candidate MST
(arXiv:2509.05527). Champions: arXiv:0810.0444 organizers and working-group chairs Buckley,
Byrum, Dingus, Falcone, Kaaret, Krawczynski, Pohl, Vassiliev, Williams; the appended workshop
programme names Vassiliev, Swordy and Byrum as session chairpersons. Later: Rene Ong
represented the CTA Consortium (arXiv:1709.05434, 2017) and led the U.S. site study
(arXiv:1307.4719, 2013); Humensky and Vassiliev on the pSCT papers (arXiv:1709.06324,
arXiv:2110.07463). Hofmann and Zanin (arXiv:2305.12888) are CTAO, not U.S., and are not
rowed. Ong's co-spokesperson title is a snippet only.

### astro2010-ccat

Agency: NSF funder (NWNH ES.2, $37M of $140M plus $7.5M/yr operations). Midterm chapter 3
(2016): NSF design support $4.75M 2011-2015; Portfolio Review conditioned CCAT on MSIP at
$30M/yr; MSIP proposal not funded; project being rebaselined. Champions: arXiv:0704.3031
(2007, Radford, Giovanelli, Sebring, Zmuidzinas) and arXiv:astro-ph/0610528 (SPIE 2006,
Sebring, Giovanelli, Radford, Zmuidzinas; feasibility study "under the leadership of a Project
Manager and Deputy", unnamed). The 2009 Astro2010 white paper author list (Giovanelli,
Carpenter, Radford, Sebring, Soifer, Stacey, Zmuidzinas and the CCAT Collaboration) is a
search snippet from submm.caltech.edu, so Giovanelli's lead-author row and Sebring's project
manager title are `guess`. Later: Stacey lead author of CCAT-prime science (arXiv:1807.04354,
2018); Niemack telescope design (Applied Optics 2016, cited in arXiv:2608.25111); Vavagiakis
lead author for the CCAT Collaboration (arXiv:2511.01707, arXiv:2608.25111), "led by Cornell
University".
